The Problem

Kazakhstan holds of foreign direct investment, but outside oil, gas and mining the new money is narrow and sits close to the resource base.

Scope

"Outside the resource value chain" can be read two ways. Narrow: investment in activities that do not process the country's own hydrocarbons or ores, so metallurgy, refining and petrochemicals do not count. Broad (the official reading): moving up the value chain into midstream and downstream processing counts as diversification. This site reports both, and the gap between them is part of the finding.

Who is affected

The state's diversification agenda, which the OECD finds only partly fulfilled; firms and workers in non-resource sectors that would gain from foreign capital, technology and supplier links. [TO FILL: regions or groups, only if I can evidence them]

The social-science question

Why does foreign capital outside the resource value chain stay small and resource-adjacent in Kazakhstan? Is the binding constraint information and matching frictions (H1), crowding-out by the resource sector (H2), or weak credible commitment and state discretion (H3)? The AI question comes second: under what conditions would an AI investor-targeting tool address it?

  • H0 Market size, distance and factor costs explain the pattern. I collected no data on this, so the site cannot rule it out.
  • H1 Information and matching frictions. This is the only hypothesis an AI tool could touch, and I found no public evidence for it.
  • H2 Crowding-out by the resource sector (Dutch disease, competition for rents).
  • H3 Weak credible commitment and state discretion.

Current State of Research

Research agrees that resource wealth shapes who invests and on what terms, but it disagrees on whether promotion, linkages or institutions are the lever.

The gap

In a non-systematic search, I found (i) no Kazakhstan-specific evidence on whether investors outside resources stall at partner, site or supplier matching; (ii) no causal evaluation of AI-based investor targeting; and (iii) investment-promotion estimates only from small open economies, chiefly Costa Rica, which cannot separate information frictions from rent and commitment constraints in a state-heavy resource economy. I did not search systematically in Scopus, Google Scholar or Russian-language literature.

The AI-Assisted Solution

An AI tool that ranks foreign firms and sectors by their likelihood to invest outside the resource base, to help the national investment promotion agency prioritise outreach.

What it does
Ranks candidate firms and sectors for outreach. A human officer reads the ranking and decides.
Inputs
Public firm and sector data, public project announcements, relatedness data; [TO FILL: whether any non-public agency records would be used, and under what consent].
Output
A ranked list with the reasons for each rank.
Who decides
The agency's staff, not the model. [TO FILL: oversight arrangement]
How it is evaluated
Against a holdout group and a non-AI baseline, with an objective that rewards sector novelty and not resemblance to past investors.
What it does not touch
Energy prices, courts, tax rules and market size (H0, H2, H3).
